# Superbuy Warehouse Storage: Free Period, Fees and Deadlines

Superbuy warehouse storage gives you time to review purchased items and plan a parcel, but it needs a deadline for each item. This guide explains the published free period, the difference between paid storage and an extension, and a practical method for deciding whether waiting for more products still makes sense. Your live warehouse record and current service terms govern the actual deadline and charges.

## Keep three storage concepts separate

The official fee page currently describes a 90-day free storage period, a daily charge after that allowance and a standard maximum storage period of 180 days. It also lists a paid extension service. These are three different ideas: time with no ordinary storage charge, time that may incur a daily charge, and permission to retain an item beyond its ordinary maximum. Do not treat payment for one as automatically covering the others.

Create separate columns for the free-storage deadline, the maximum deadline, the current charge and any approved extension. Use dates shown for the actual warehouse item when available. If the interface or an item-specific notice differs from a general guide, ask support to clarify before relying on the later date. A private reminder helps you manage the record; it does not change the platform's clock.

## Record arrival information for each item

Several items purchased together can reach the warehouse on different dates. Keep a row for every stored item with its warehouse identifier, order identifier, received or stored date and current deadline. Do not assign the newest item's free period to the entire group. A consolidation plan usually needs special attention to the oldest item, because it reaches the next decision point first.

Distinguish the domestic carrier's delivery scan from the date or deadline actually shown in the warehouse account. If the storage-start rule is unclear, ask which date applies to that item instead of choosing the one that creates more free days. Save the answer in your private record. This is especially useful when processing, a split order or an unmatched incoming parcel makes the timeline less obvious.

## Review the item before planning a long wait

Storage is most useful after you understand what is being stored. Confirm identity, selected colour and size, quantity, included pieces and visible condition. Review the available QC photographs and obtain a necessary measurement while the relevant after-sales option remains available. A generous storage allowance does not extend a seller's return window.

Mark each item accept, clarify or after-sales. Accepted items can enter a parcel comparison. An item with an unresolved defect or option mismatch should remain a separate action until you have the evidence or resolution you need. Do not postpone a QC decision merely because more products are on the way. The cost of losing an available remedy can outweigh any later benefit from combining shipments.

## Estimate daily charges with the correct unit

The published fee information lists an ordinary post-free-period charge of CNY 0.10 per item per day. Check the current interface and how it counts the stored unit for your order. A multi-piece set, a split order and several separate items may not be interchangeable for billing. Treat the account's applicable unit and displayed amount as the basis for the final calculation.

For illustration only, one billable item kept for 12 chargeable days at CNY 0.10 per day would add CNY 1.20. Four separately billable items over the same period would add CNY 4.80. This arithmetic is not a confirmation of your storage dates or a promise of eligibility. Record the actual days and units, then compare the estimate with the charge shown when you submit the parcel.

## Understand what an extension changes

Superbuy's fee page lists storage extension at CNY 10 per order per month, with a stated limit of six months for an individual product. The description says the service extends the maximum storage period and that daily charges still apply after free storage ends. Confirm the applicable unit, eligible item and duration in your account before selecting it; do not assume an extension creates a new free period.

Save the confirmation and check that the item's displayed deadline changes as expected. If it does not, resolve the discrepancy before the original deadline. Extension is a specific service for keeping an item longer, not a reason to leave an unresolved return or an unshippable product unattended. Ask what action is required when an item cannot be dispatched before its limit; do not infer a grace period from silence.

## Compare waiting with shipping now

A useful waiting decision compares the cost of shipping accepted items now with the cost and risk of waiting for the remaining products. Include any confirmed daily storage charges, an extension if needed, changes in parcel dimensions and the possibility that a late item restricts available routes. A lower repeated base charge is only one part of the comparison.

Write down what you expect to gain by waiting and what event will end the wait. For example, you might wait for a seller-dispatched garment that is compatible with the existing parcel, while setting a review date before the oldest stored item's deadline. If the seller has not dispatched or cannot confirm availability, compare a split shipment rather than repeatedly moving the plan without new evidence.

## Group items by compatibility as well as date

Use a parcel group for products that can travel together on an eligible route and receive suitable protection. Fragile items, batteries, liquids, oversized packaging and shaped goods deserve individual attention. One unsuitable item can change the handling or route options for everything else. Check current item labels and route conditions instead of relying on a broad category name in a spreadsheet.

List the proposed packing changes next to each group. Removing unnecessary packaging can reduce volume, but it can also affect protection or return eligibility. Keep items with open after-sales questions out of irreversible packing operations. Where two parcel plans are plausible, compare both with current dimensions and route inputs. A clear group definition makes the final selection easier to verify before submission.

## Use reminders that lead to a decision

A reminder should identify the item and the required action, not just announce that a date is approaching. Useful actions include review QC, answer an agent message, confirm route eligibility, submit a parcel or check an approved extension. Choose your private reminder dates early enough to leave room for these steps. Your chosen reminder schedule is a planning method, not an official Superbuy deadline.

A weekly review can sort by the nearest free-storage deadline, the nearest maximum deadline and unresolved buyer actions. For each row, record the next owner and the evidence needed to close the task. If a plan depends on another seller, give that dependency a review date. This prevents a warehouse list from becoming a collection of indefinite waiting states.

## Check the account before the final submission

Before parcel payment, verify that every selected item is accepted, belongs in the group and can use the selected route. Check any storage charge, packing service and extension entry shown in the process. Preserve the estimate privately, then reconcile it after the final parcel charge is available. Do not remove a row from your tracker just because it is selected in a draft parcel.

Confirm the later dispatch or relevant completion event before closing the storage task. If the parcel is returned to the warehouse or an item is excluded, reopen the record and check its current deadline. Do not assume the free period restarts automatically. A change in parcel status deserves a fresh reading of the actual item record and any instructions attached to it.

## Keep storage discipline in the next purchase

Before adding another product, check whether it fits the parcel and timeline you already have. A low reference price can be misleading if the item delays the shipment, needs a different route or introduces a costly packing requirement. Review your oldest stored item first, then decide whether the new purchase improves the plan or simply postpones it.

After the parcel leaves, keep the arrival dates, waiting reason, actual storage charges and final outcome. Use this record to improve the next consolidation decision. The goal is not to use every free day; it is to preserve useful time for inspection and deliberate shipping while avoiding missed deadlines. Pair this routine with the order tracker and shipping guide so that product, warehouse and parcel decisions stay connected.
