Find the two figures you want to compare

A freight estimate and a payment receipt can describe different stages of one purchase. Start with the original amount beside the name of the international shipping service. Match it to the international freight line on the final parcel statement. Put delivery from the seller to the Chinese warehouse in its own row. Put any amount paid on delivery in a separate row too. You can then answer two different questions: how much international freight changed, and how much the whole purchase cost.

Date both records. On 5 November 2026 the official AllChinaBuy homepage displayed a maintenance notice. The historical calculator address is a reference for readers with saved estimates. If you have an open parcel, use the service and terms printed on your own documents. The examples below use fictional charges so you can follow the arithmetic with your own figures.

Rebuild the estimate on one sheet

Write one row per parcel. If you have three seller order numbers but one international parcel number, add the three items to the contents field of the same row. If the warehouse split the consignment, make two rows, each with its own carton, route and base fee. Comparing the total weight of all the goods with a charge for just one carton produces a difference that repacking cannot explain.

Keep the original currency for your first comparison. A card statement converted into euros can move even if the amount in yuan stayed the same. Once you have reconciled the invoice currency, compare the conversion and payment fee separately. If your estimate preserved only a total without a route or measurements, mark those fields as unknown. You can still identify documented differences without assigning the remainder to a fee you cannot see.

Which weight set the charge?

Weigh the entire closed parcel with its carton and protection. Measure the greatest outside length, width and height. An item description that gives the dimensions of a folded jacket or the weight of a single shoe supplies a different input. For soft mailers, a bulging corner can determine the outside measurement. Note whether a figure in your record is in grams or kilograms before copying it across.

A line that uses dimensional weight converts the packed volume into a weight equivalent. With dimensions in centimetres and a divisor of 5,000, the weight in kilograms equals length × width × height ÷ 5,000. Compare the result with scale weight if the line charges the larger figure. Use the divisor, rounding order, weight increments and minimum on your route. DHL describes 5,000 as its standard metric divisor in its weight explanation. UPS shows how rounding can vary by service in its Singapore parcel measurement guide. Neither document establishes the terms of a particular AllChinaBuy booking.

Example 1: more space, same contents

Suppose a historical estimate used a 40 × 30 × 15 cm carton and a packed weight of 3.4 kg. The final carton measures 40 × 30 × 20 cm and weighs 3.6 kg. For this exercise the route charges the larger of physical and dimensional weight, divides volume by 5,000, and rounds the result up to the next 0.5 kg. Keep the destination, service, rate and currency identical throughout the exercise.

The initial dimensional weight is 3.6 kg, which exceeds 3.4 kg on the scale. The billed band is therefore 4.0 kg. After packing, dimensional weight is 4.8 kg, which exceeds 3.6 kg on the scale. The final billed band is 5.0 kg. Five extra centimetres of carton height have pushed the parcel up two half-kilogram increments, although the scale shows an increase of only 200 g.

Use a fictional tariff of CNY 50 for the first 0.5 kg plus CNY 8 per additional 0.5 kg. At 4.0 kg the calculation is 50 + 7 × 8 = CNY 106. At 5.0 kg it is 50 + 9 × 8 = CNY 122. The CNY 16 difference is fully explained by the billed bands. Before deciding to remove a shoe box, ask for the proposed outside dimensions and how the shoes would be protected. A smaller retail box changes the calculation only if it changes the final parcel measurements or weight band.

Example 2: crossing a band on the scale

Consider a compact parcel measuring 30 × 20 × 10 cm in both records. With the same divisor its dimensional weight stays at 1.2 kg. The estimate uses a scale weight of 1.92 kg. The sealed parcel weighs 2.08 kg. Under the example rule, scale weight controls both times and the billed weight moves from 2.0 to 2.5 kg. This time a smaller carton alone would leave the price unchanged if the parcel still weighed 2.08 kg.

At the fictional tariff above, the freight amount moves from 50 + 3 × 8 = CNY 74 to 50 + 4 × 8 = CNY 82. Suppose the final statement also lists CNY 6 for requested packing and shows a CNY 10 credit applied. The total after credit is 82 + 6 − 10 = CNY 78. Against the original CNY 74 estimate, the net difference is CNY 4. The transport charge itself rose CNY 8.

A receipt might show only the balance due. If CNY 60 had already been paid towards that CNY 78 total, the remaining payment would be CNY 18. Adding the earlier payment to the total again would count the deposit twice. Copy the labels for “freight”, “credit”, “paid” and “balance” exactly as they appear before you compare the numbers.

When the arithmetic still leaves a difference

Check the service name, service code and destination postcode on both documents. A switch of route can change both the rate and the weight rule. Recalculate each quotation with its own terms before blaming the dimensions. If the documents have different dates, check for a dated rate change or a change in the currency used. A screenshot of someone else's shipment cannot identify which rule applied to yours.

Next, compare the included services. Look for an itemized charge for packing or protection, a separate carrier surcharge, or a discount that applied to one quote. Record only amounts actually shown. Split shipments can incur two first-weight charges, so reconcile each carton individually before adding the totals. Keep any customs assessment or collection receipt alongside the freight statement with its own date, currency and payee.

If a difference remains, ask one specific question about the missing evidence. For the first example, a useful request would be: “My copy of the estimate shows a 15 cm carton height; the final record shows 20 cm. Could you confirm the outside dimensions used and the resulting billed band for parcel [reference]?” Attach the dated estimate and the relevant lines of the final statement. Hide addresses and payment details when sharing records publicly. Save the reply alongside both figures so a later credit or adjustment can be linked to the correct parcel.
