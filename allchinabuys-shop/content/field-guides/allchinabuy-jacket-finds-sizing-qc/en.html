Two jacket cards can lead to one seller listing

The jacket category linked from this directory gives a good example. Open Jackets-59 and Jackets-60 side by side. Both detail cards display Weidian item ID 7672835699. The seller page shows a starting price. The two card names alone cannot tell you which colour, lining or size that price buys. Treat them as two leads to the same listing until you can distinguish the actual options.

In your shortlist, save one row per option you can identify. Copy the seller link, the exact option text or code, its matching thumbnail, the size, the price displayed after selection and the date. If the selector changes the photo or price, save the new state. If you can see only the headline and starting price, leave the other fields blank. This keeps two screenshots of the same jacket from turning into a misleading price comparison.

Start with the layer you will wear underneath

Try on a jacket you already own over the clothes you intend to wear. Zip it up, reach forward as if holding handlebars and bend your elbows. Note precisely where it pulls. If your reference jacket fits over a T-shirt but binds at the upper arm over a fleece, matching only its chest measurement could reproduce the same problem. Measure a different reference if you need more room for layering.

For a puffer, compare with another padded jacket of similar construction. Two jackets can have the same outside width and leave different amounts of space inside because their insulation and lining occupy different volumes. On a short bomber, the position and tension of the hem can matter more than an extra centimetre of back length. With a rain shell, check whether the cuffs still cover the wrists when you reach forward. These observations tell you which measurements deserve attention first.

Compare the same measurement on both jackets

Fasten your reference jacket, empty the pockets and lay it on a level surface. Smooth obvious folds without pulling the fabric or flattening the padding. Use a flexible tape and record the unit. A photo of your tape positions is helpful when you receive a seller chart labelled simply “length.” The UNIQLO garment measurement guide illustrates why the measurement origin matters, especially for sleeves and back length. Apply the seller's own illustrated method when comparing that seller's numbers.

What to compare Where to measure When to ask for clarification
Flat chest width Straight between underarm points on the closed jacket The chart says “chest” without indicating flat width, garment circumference or body size
Back length Along the centre back from the base of the collar to the hem One measurement begins at the shoulder or includes the collar
Sleeve reach Shoulder seam to cuff on a set-in sleeve, or centre back to cuff if specified One jacket has raglan or dropped shoulders and the two charts use different start points
Hem and upper arm Across the relaxed hem and across the sleeve at a stated distance below the armhole Elastic is stretched in one photo or the tape positions differ

A garment's flat width can be doubled for an approximate circumference if that is how the chart defines it. Your own body circumference is a different measurement. When a seller gives a recommended chest range for the wearer, request the garment width for the exact size. Do not add a standard number of centimetres to force the two charts to agree. Use the layering test to decide how much difference you can accept.

A worked sizing decision

Consider a hypothetical lined jacket for cycling to work. Your reference measures 58 cm across the chest, 68 cm down the centre back and 85 cm from centre back to cuff. A candidate lists 116 cm chest, 69 cm length and 63 cm sleeve. These figures make a reasonable first match only after the seller confirms that 116 cm is the finished garment circumference and that the length uses the same starting point. The corresponding flat chest would then be 58 cm.

The sleeve still needs a check. A 63 cm shoulder-to-cuff length and an 85 cm centre-back-to-cuff length describe different paths. Subtracting them gives no useful fit information. Ask for the candidate measured from centre back to cuff with the jacket lying flat. If the seller can confirm only the shoulder-to-cuff method, take that same measurement on your reference. Record the result with the size code. Changing to another size solely to make the sleeve figures look closer could undo the chest fit you just established.

Inspect the received jacket in a useful order

Where you have access to order-specific warehouse QC photos, begin with the front and back laid out, the option label and the inside of the jacket. Compare the pocket positions, hood or collar shape and lining with your saved option photo. A colour name such as charcoal can cover several shades. If colour matters, ask for an evenly lit picture beside a neutral reference and allow for differences between lighting and screens.

Next request one photo for each uncertain detail. For a measurement, specify the two endpoints and ask that the tape's zero and final reading both appear. For a zipper, ask for a close-up of the bottom box, slider and teeth. If function is important and the service permits it, request a short opening-and-closing check. A still photo can show missing teeth but cannot establish smooth operation along the whole track. For a puffer, inspect front and back panels for a visibly empty area, torn seam or trapped lining. A photograph alone cannot determine fill composition, warmth rating or water resistance.

Keep the question connected to the order. “Please photograph the width directly below the underarms on the closed size M, with the tape ends visible” gives a more useful answer than “please check size.” If the wrong option label appears, resolve that first. More measurements of the wrong garment add little value. Save the reply and the date alongside your original selection so a later enquiry refers to one item.

Plan the parcel after the clothing check

Ask separately for the jacket alone, the parcel on the scale and the outside dimensions after the proposed packing. A product-card number with no unit or weighing method is a poor basis for postage. A light puffer can occupy more space than a heavier, neatly folded work jacket. A stiff brim, structured shoulders or delicate trims may also change which packing method is acceptable.

For a hypothetical parcel measuring 40 × 30 × 15 cm and weighing 1.2 kg, a route using a divisor of 5,000 gives a dimensional weight of 3.6 kg. A route charging the greater of scale and dimensional weight would start from 3.6 kg before its own rounding and fees. DHL Express explains this calculation for its service. Enter the actual dimensions and read the conditions of the route quoted for your destination. When comparing two packing proposals, keep the contents and destination fixed and confirm that each proposal provides the protection the garment needs.

Keep the selection only when your records explain which option you chose, how it compares with the jacket you wear, what the inspection shows and how the parcel estimate was obtained. If one of those answers is missing, record the precise question and return to it before approving an order or shipment.
