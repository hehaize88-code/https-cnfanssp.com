How to read AllChinaBuy warehouse QC photos before you submit a parcel

The official service includes inspection and photography, but the buyer still decides what the images prove. Use this repeatable checklist and request extra evidence when a detail matters.

Editorial standardOfficial platform pages checked August 26, 2026. User-review observations are labeled separately and are not treated as guarantees.

QC means inspection evidence, not certification

Warehouse photographs are commonly called QC photos because they give the buyer a chance to inspect the received item before international shipment. The phrase can be misleading when a spreadsheet labels every product “QC approved.” A photo by itself does not approve anything. It shows part of the item under particular lighting, at a particular resolution and from a limited number of angles. The buyer still needs to compare that evidence with the listing and decide whether the item is acceptable.

Begin with the order, not with minor details. Confirm that the warehouse record belongs to the correct item, seller and selected variant. Check the color, size label, quantity and included pieces. A perfectly photographed wrong variant is still the wrong order.

AllChinaBuy’s official homepage places “Quality Check & Storage” between order payment and international logistics. It separately promotes quality inspection, photography and 90-day free storage. That supports a sensible workflow: wait for the warehouse record, inspect the evidence, resolve a material problem while the order is still eligible for after-sales handling, and only then place the item into an international parcel.

Use a fixed order

Identity → measurements → overall shape → alignment → materials → visible defects → packaging. A fixed sequence reduces the chance that an eye-catching detail distracts you from a basic error.

1. Confirm identity and options

Match the warehouse images against the exact option selected during purchase. Multi-style product pages often reuse the same main image, so confirm the option code or thumbnail as well as the product name. For sets, count the pieces. For accessories, confirm hardware color and size. For shoes, check that both shoes are the same model, color and size.

Lighting can change apparent color. Compare several images and look at neutral objects in the frame. If the background or measurement ruler has a strong color cast, the product color may also appear warmer or cooler than it is. Request a daylight or closer image only when color accuracy is central to the decision.

2. Check measurements before aesthetics

For clothing, a measurement photo can be more valuable than several close-ups. Compare chest width, length, waist or inseam with the size chart and with a garment you already own. Make sure the tape begins and ends at the correct points. A folded edge, curved tape or thick garment can affect the result.

For footwear, an insole measurement is useful when the chart is unclear. Outsole length is not the same as internal fit. Ask which measurement is shown and leave enough room for the intended fit. If the warehouse cannot provide the exact measurement you need, recognize the remaining uncertainty instead of treating a generic size label as proof.

3. Review overall shape and symmetry

Step back before zooming in. Compare left and right sides, panel shape, collar height, toe shape, sleeve length and the way the item sits when laid flat. Mild differences can come from positioning, so use more than one angle. Large asymmetry that remains across images deserves attention.

Shoes should be viewed as a pair. Compare toe boxes, heel height, sole curvature and the placement of corresponding panels. For jackets and hoodies, compare pocket height, shoulder shape, zipper line and whether printed or embroidered elements sit consistently relative to seams.

4. Inspect alignment and construction

Look at seams, edges, panel joins and repeated elements. The goal is not to find microscopic imperfections in a low-resolution image; it is to identify defects that will be visible during normal use or that suggest a construction problem. Loose threads can be minor, while an open seam, crooked zipper or badly shifted panel may justify an after-sales request.

Printed and embroidered elements should be evaluated for placement, scale and clean edges. Compare them with the listing image cautiously because seller images may be edited or may show a different sample. The most useful comparison is whether the received item matches the product and option you deliberately chose.

5. Evaluate materials without pretending photos tell everything

Photos can show surface texture, shine, thickness at an edge and the way fabric folds, but they cannot reliably prove fiber content, long-term durability, smell or comfort. Avoid absolute statements such as “premium leather” or “heavy cotton” unless the listing provides evidence and the received item can be measured accordingly.

When fabric weight matters, request the item weight and compare it with similar garments. For bags or shoes, a close-up may reveal grain and stitching, but it still cannot confirm how the material will age. A useful spreadsheet states what can be observed and leaves unverified qualities unclaimed.

6. Look for functional and visible defects

Common checks include stains, scratches, glue marks, damaged hardware, broken closures, missing accessories, holes, open seams and obvious deformation. Ask for a close-up if a standard photo suggests a problem but is inconclusive. Mark the area in your request so the warehouse knows what to photograph.

For electronics, a visual photo is not a functional test. Check whether the order offers a testing service and what that service actually covers. Battery condition, internal components, network compatibility and long-term reliability cannot be proven by an exterior image.

7. Decide: accept, request evidence or use after-sales

There are three sensible outcomes. Accept when the item and variant match and no material issue is visible. Request another photo or measurement when the available evidence cannot answer a specific question. Use the order’s available return or exchange process when there is a clear mismatch or defect and the seller’s terms allow it.

Do not let an approaching parcel shipment push you into ignoring a clear problem. At the same time, do not expect warehouse photos to resolve every subjective preference. Decide which issues are material before ordering and write those checks down. That makes the final decision faster and more consistent.

What customer reports add to the checklist

User reports are mixed, which is exactly why a checklist is more useful than a blanket recommendation. In the Trustpilot snapshot checked on August 26, 2026, AllChinaBuy showed a 4.1 score from 214 reviews, with 74% five-star and 22% one-star reviews. Positive reports often mentioned responsive support, quick warehouse updates and timely QC photos. Negative reports included disputes about sizing evidence, seller cooperation, documentation and shipping transparency. These are individual experiences, not controlled tests, but they identify the moments where good records matter most.

Reddit discussions show the same spread. Some buyers describe warehouse arrival and QC in roughly a week, while others report longer waits or difficult problem resolution. Do not convert those anecdotes into a promised processing time. Use them to plan: keep screenshots, review order status, ask a specific question, and avoid submitting the parcel until the evidence is sufficient.

How to write a useful extra-photo request

A vague request such as “check quality” can produce a vague result. Identify one location and one question: measure the insole from heel to toe; photograph the left sleeve print straight-on; show the zipper closed; place the measuring tape across the chest below the armholes; photograph the scratch beside a neutral ruler. A request that can be completed in one frame is easier to interpret.

Keep the requested proof proportional to the decision. If a two-centimeter garment difference changes fit, a measurement is material. If a tiny loose thread can be trimmed and would not affect the decision, multiple paid photographs may not add value. The objective is not to collect the maximum number of images. It is to reduce the uncertainty that could change accept-versus-return.

After-sales timing and evidence

The official site provides help categories for returns and refunds, inspection information and after-sales policy, but the result can depend on the marketplace seller, order status and current deadline. A warehouse is an intermediary, so the seller may need to approve a return or exchange. Report a mismatch as soon as it is visible and attach the clearest photo, the selected option and the relevant size chart or listing image.

Do not assume that international parcel insurance covers dissatisfaction with a product. Product after-sales and parcel loss or damage are different processes. Resolve visible product issues before shipping; read the current insurance and compensation terms when choosing the parcel line.

QC checklist to save

  • Correct product, color, size, quantity and included pieces
  • Measurements compared with the correct size chart
  • Overall shape and left-right symmetry
  • Seams, panels, zipper, pockets and repeated details
  • Print or embroidery placement and edge quality
  • Visible stains, scratches, glue, damage or missing parts
  • Packaging needs for fragile, structured or moisture-sensitive items
