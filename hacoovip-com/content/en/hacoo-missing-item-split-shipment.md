## Start with the order and the parcel separately
A Hacoo missing item is not the same problem as an entire parcel that has not arrived. First identify what is absent: a whole package, one product inside a received package, part of a set, or an accessory shown in the selected listing. These situations call for different checks. Keep the order number and tracking number separate because one order may involve more than one shipment, and a delivered tracking number does not necessarily settle every item in that order.

Hacoo’s shipping information says in-stock items and pre-order items may be sent separately. That makes a split shipment a real possibility, but it is not proof that your missing product is on its way. Read the item-level details available in your own order. If those details do not connect products to packages, ask support to clarify the allocation. Avoid assuming that a general “delivered” label means all products were inside the parcel you just opened.
## Make a simple order-to-package record
Create one line for each selected product and variant. Record the ordered quantity, any cancellation or refund, the visible shipment reference, the tracking status and the quantity physically received. Use item names that distinguish variants: “blue hoodie, size M, one” is clearer than “hoodie”. Where the interface provides only one tracking number, record that fact rather than inventing a second package reference for the missing product.

Then list each parcel separately with its arrival date and contents. This small reconciliation often exposes a quantity mismatch, an already-cancelled line or a second shipment still in transit. Screenshots should retain enough context to show the selected order and item, while keeping personal details private when shared outside support. If the order page later changes, your dated record helps explain what was visible when you first reported the problem. Do not edit screenshots to suggest a package allocation that the interface never displayed.
## Recognise evidence of a split shipment
Useful signs of a split shipment include separate tracking numbers, different dispatch dates, an item explicitly marked as pending, or a message connecting particular products to different packages. Check each available tracking number independently. An order-level progress indicator can be too broad to answer whether a particular item has left the seller, reached a local carrier or been delivered to you.

If the missing line is assigned to a second package that remains in transit, follow that package’s tracking and the estimate shown for the order. If all listed packages are delivered and the received quantity is still short, gather missing-item evidence promptly. Where the interface is ambiguous, ask a narrow question: “Which tracking number contains item X, quantity one?” Do not rely on a blanket instruction to wait several extra days when the request deadline is approaching. Clarification and preserving your right to submit a timely request can happen together.
## Check the packaging without discarding evidence
Before throwing anything away, inspect the outer packaging, inner product bags, packing list and invoice. Small accessories can remain in a pocket or a separate inner bag, while a set may be packed in more than one layer. Compare the received contents with the selected listing and quantity. A promotional photograph can show several pieces even when the purchased option contains only one, so the actual selected option matters more than the largest image.

Photograph the exterior of the parcel, its label, the available packing documents and all received contents together. Record any opened seam, damaged packaging or resealing that you observed, without claiming to know where it happened. An unboxing video can provide context if you already have one, but do not stage a reconstruction and present it as the original opening. Do not post address labels or barcodes publicly. Preserve the originals for the private order-support process.
## Use a worked example to avoid double counting
Suppose an order contains one pair of shoes, one hoodie and two accessories. Parcel A arrives with the shoes and hoodie. Parcel B is still in transit and the order explicitly assigns both accessories to B. The missing contents of A are not automatically a missing-item claim, because A’s recorded allocation is complete. Save the allocation and check B rather than counting the two unreceived accessories as losses from A.

Now change the example: B arrives with one accessory, although its allocation shows two, and no cancellation or refund explains the difference. Record the shortfall against B and the relevant item line. Photograph B’s packaging, documents and received accessory. These are hypothetical examples, not reports about actual Hacoo orders. Their purpose is to show why checking quantities and package allocation is more useful than relying on a single delivered badge or assuming that every product photo represents one separately shipped parcel.
## Submit a focused missing-item request
Hacoo’s help centre asks for photographs of the outer packaging and invoice for missing items. For missing accessories, it asks for a photograph of what arrived and a description of the missing part. Where an invoice was not included, say so and provide the order record and other available evidence; do not create a replacement document that looks like a seller-issued invoice. The order’s current support instructions may request additional information.

Use a concise message: identify the order, the affected product and variant, the ordered and received quantities, the package reference, and the date it arrived. Add whether another shipment is still pending. Ask for confirmation of package allocation or for review of the documented shortfall, depending on what you found. Attach relevant evidence rather than unrelated review screenshots. Keep the support response and any case reference so that a later follow-up can refer to the same issue instead of creating contradictory accounts.
## Separate review, return instructions and refund timing
Reporting a missing item does not mean a refund has already been approved. The public refund policy describes a general 15-day window after receipt for eligible requests; product and case conditions still matter. Contact support promptly when shipment allocation is unclear, particularly if part of the order has already been delivered. Do not assume that a pending second shipment automatically extends every deadline attached to the first parcel.

If the case also involves a wrong or damaged product, follow the instructions for that item. Some eligible cases can receive a refund without a return, while others require a return. Do not send back received goods on the assumption that doing so will resolve the missing product. When a refund is approved and processed, payment posting is a further stage. Keep the approval, amount and payment route together, then use the current order information and refund policy to assess a delay.
## Choose the next guide for the actual problem
If nothing has arrived, begin with the order-tracking guide and the last carrier event. If a parcel arrived with the wrong product, preserve the product, label and package evidence and use the returns evidence guide. If the correct product arrived but its size or condition is the concern, use the QC photo and size guides. Keeping the categories distinct makes your evidence easier to review and avoids turning a delivery question into an unrelated quality complaint.

Before closing your own record, reconcile every ordered line with a received item, a pending shipment, an explicit cancellation or a confirmed resolution. Mark unresolved entries as unresolved. A useful final checklist is simple: all tracking references checked, quantities compared, packaging retained, evidence saved and the appropriate order request submitted on time. It will not guarantee a particular decision, but it gives support the concrete information needed to investigate what actually happened.
