## What a useful sneaker QC check can answer
A Hacoo sneaker QC check starts with three separate questions: is this the selected variant, are there visible problems, and is there enough size information to make a decision? Keep those questions separate. A clean side photograph can help you compare shape or colour, but it cannot tell you how much usable space is inside the shoe. A size label identifies what was labelled; it does not establish that the fit matches another brand’s size chart.

Begin with the saved listing, the selected colour and size, and the order item reference. If you have only a catalogue image, record that limitation and ask for the missing information through whatever contact or order options are actually available. Do not describe catalogue photos as an inspection of your pair. This guide provides a repeatable way to review evidence, not a promise that every Hacoo listing offers warehouse photographs or a pre-dispatch QC service.
## Match the photographs to the exact pair
Before examining stitching, make sure the photographs belong to the item you are assessing. Compare the selected variant with the size label, colour blocking, sole pattern and any visible product reference. Where a seller offers multiple styles in one listing, save the selected option as well as the main page. A similar shoe shown in the hero image may be a different variant from the one in the basket.

Keep the full original photograph and use a cropped copy only for a specific detail. Ask when the picture was taken and which item it represents if the connection is unclear. A label, packaging or order reference can help establish that connection, but do not publish your address, tracking barcode or account details. If two photographs show different size labels, stop the comparison until that discrepancy is resolved. More photographs are useful only when they can be linked to the same pair and selected size.
## Use a practical six-view photo list
For an ordinary visual review, request or organise six groups of views: both shoes from above, each outer side, each inner side, both heels at the same height, the soles, and both size labels. Add a close photograph of any suspected problem. Pairing the shoes in one frame makes obvious differences in toe shape, heel height or colour easier to see than comparing pictures taken from unrelated angles.

Use even light and keep the camera approximately square to the surface being compared. A wide-angle photograph taken very close to one shoe can exaggerate its length. Heavy shadows can resemble glue marks, and bright reflections can hide surface damage. Ask for a second angle before treating an ambiguous patch as a defect. This visual list helps structure questions; it cannot confirm material composition, authenticity, comfort or durability. Those claims need different evidence, and a polished image does not supply it.
## Measure a removable insole consistently
If the insole is designed to come out without damage, place it flat beside a straight ruler on a firm surface. Photograph the entire insole with the ruler markings readable. Start the measurement at the ruler’s zero mark rather than at the physical edge of the ruler. Record the straight heel-to-toe length and, if relevant, the width across the widest forefoot area. Keep the unit visible and use centimetres or millimetres consistently.

Do not force out a glued insole or flatten a shaped footbed in a way that changes its form. A tape draped over a curve measures a different path from a straight ruler. If the insole cannot be removed, state that the available measurement uses another method and avoid comparing the numbers as though they were equivalent. Ideally measure both shoes and photograph each result. A difference should prompt a repeat measurement using the same setup before you decide that the pair itself is inconsistent.
## Compare with a pair that already fits
Measure a comfortable pair of a similar type using the same method. Comparing two insoles measured alike is often more useful than comparing an isolated number with a generic size conversion chart. Also note the reference shoe’s width, toe shape, lining and how you use it. A close numerical match does not guarantee equal usable space when one shoe has a narrower toe box, a thicker lining or a more curved footbed.

Here is a hypothetical example, not a measurement of the pictured shoe: your reference insole measures 27.2 cm and the candidate photograph appears to show 27.0 cm. The apparent difference is 2 mm. If the ruler’s zero point is hidden or the camera is angled, that photograph does not justify a precise conclusion about those 2 mm. Request a clearer view, compare the widths and consult the selected variant’s own size information. Do not turn this example into a universal size-up rule or a recommended allowance for every foot.
## Distinguish a visible defect from a preference
Write down observations before conclusions. “The left heel appears higher in this view” is a useful observation; “the construction is poor” needs more evidence. Recheck symmetry with both shoes placed level, then look for detached components, open stitching, tears, stains or mismatched labels. Cosmetic preferences such as a particular toe shape should be compared with the selected listing rather than treated automatically as a product fault.

Use a short decision record with four fields: feature, visible evidence, uncertainty and next action. For example, a dark line along the sole might need a daylight close-up, while a clearly different size label needs clarification of the supplied variant. Mark an unphotographed area as unknown instead of giving it a passing score. This prevents a long photo collection from creating false confidence and makes a follow-up request more specific than asking whether the shoes are simply “good”.
## Prepare useful evidence after delivery
If the delivered pair has a size or quality problem, keep the packaging and photograph the item before wearing, washing or altering it. Hacoo’s help centre asks for size-label and product-bag barcode evidence for size problems, alongside photographs showing the issue. For quality problems, it asks for photographs of the defective area. Retain an overall view too, so a close-up can be connected to the received item.

Open the affected order promptly and follow the instructions shown there. The public refund policy describes a general 15-day request window for eligible items, with category and condition restrictions. A photo checklist does not extend that window or guarantee approval. Send the relevant evidence privately through the order’s support process, and keep a copy of your request. Do not mail the shoes to an address inferred from a shipping label; obtain the return instructions applicable to that particular case first.
## Turn the check into one clear next step
Finish with one of three outcomes: enough information to continue comparing, a specific missing view or measurement to request, or a documented issue to raise through the order. Keep the wording narrow. “Need a readable ruler zero point” tells someone what to fix; “need better QC” leaves the task undefined. Save the date, selected variant and photographs together so that later changes to a listing do not erase the context of your decision.
