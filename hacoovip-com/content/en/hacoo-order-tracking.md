## Start with the order, then identify each parcel
For Hacoo order tracking, begin with the order you actually placed. Open its current details in the app or service where you paid and look for the shipment information supplied for that order. Labels and menu locations can change, so use the current order interface instead of following an old screenshot literally. Record the order date, dispatched items, carrier name if supplied, tracking number and last visible update. An order number identifies the purchase; a tracking number identifies a shipment. Entering one in a field intended for the other can produce an empty result without proving that anything is lost.

One order can also contain several parcels. Hacoo's public shipping guidance says available products may be sent before preordered or backordered items. Match every number to the items assigned to it. If a hoodie arrives but a second item does not, first check whether the second item has a separate shipment. Do not treat the first parcel's delivery scan as confirmation that the whole order was delivered.
## Read the event, its date and its location together
Common carrier descriptions include information received, accepted, in transit, customs processing, out for delivery and delivered. These are general logistics terms; Hacoo and individual carriers may use different wording. An electronic shipment record can exist before a carrier records physical receipt. An in-transit event normally means a movement or processing stage, not a promise that the parcel will arrive tomorrow. A customs message requires reading the accompanying instructions rather than guessing that a charge is always due.

Keep the exact wording and timestamp. A timeline with no new event is different from a timeline reporting a failed delivery attempt or an address problem. Also check whether the displayed date uses a different time zone. Ask a precise question when something is unclear: which parcel is affected, what was the last event, and what action does the carrier currently request? That is more actionable than saying only that tracking is broken.
## Separate the delivery estimate from the tracking timeline
An estimated arrival window and a parcel's event history answer different questions. The estimate is a planning reference; the events describe information received so far. Hacoo's public Shipping & Delivery page lists broad destination ranges and explicitly describes shipping times as guidelines. Your selected item, address and order may have different instructions. Use the estimate attached to the actual order when deciding whether to contact support, and preserve the estimate you saw when purchasing.

Do not add every processing and transport interval from different help pages to create a new promised date. Some sources use business days, others simply say days, and their stages may overlap. Nor does a fast express example prove that your order used that service. If the item is needed for an event, a tracking update is useful evidence of progress but not a substitute for a confirmed delivery commitment.
## When Hacoo tracking is not updating
A quiet tracking period alone does not establish loss. Check that the number was copied completely, that you selected the carrier identified in the order, and that you are looking at the correct parcel. Open the carrier information provided through the order when available. A handover between carriers can create a second number or a delay before the next event appears. Check for that explanation before searching random tracking services or installing an unrelated app.

Compare the current timeline with your last saved observation and the order's estimate. If the estimate has passed, the status requests action, or the same unexplained event persists beyond the guidance in the order, ask support for a parcel-specific update. There is no universal number of silent days after which every Hacoo parcel should be declared lost. Avoid inventing a deadline from another customer's experience; their route, carrier and dispatch date may be different.
## Respond promptly to address and delivery exceptions
An address issue calls for action as soon as you notice it. Hacoo's shipping page says the opportunity to correct an address can be small and advises contacting support immediately. Provide the order reference through the authenticated support channel and explain which address field is wrong. Do not assume that editing an account profile changes a parcel already dispatched. Ask whether a correction is still possible for this specific shipment and retain the reply.

For a failed delivery or collection notice, read the actual carrier instruction and any stated collection deadline. Check the building, unit number, access arrangement or pickup point relevant to your delivery. Use the contact details supplied by the order or carrier rather than an unsolicited message demanding a payment. Never post your full address, phone number, barcode or tracking number in a public review while requesting help.
## If tracking says delivered but nothing arrived
First verify that the delivered event belongs to the missing item, not another parcel. Review any delivery photo, signature or location information the carrier provides. Check the reception desk, agreed safe place, household member or neighbour only where that is relevant to the delivery arrangement. Record what you checked and when. A delivery scan is evidence of a carrier event; it does not answer every question about where the parcel was left.

Hacoo's shipping page directs readers with a delivered-but-not-received parcel to report it to the carrier and contact customer support for help. Follow the current order procedure and ask for the delivery evidence to be reviewed. Do not fabricate a missing-parcel claim or assume an automatic refund. Keep the support reference, the carrier response and any promised follow-up date together so you can continue the same case without repeatedly submitting conflicting descriptions.
## Keep missing items and damaged items separate
An empty space in a parcel and a parcel that never arrived are different problems. Compare the packing information with the order's shipment allocation before reporting a missing item. Photograph the packaging, shipping label, contents and any visible damage before discarding them. For a wrong variant, add the option label and the relevant order detail. A clear comparison between expected and received contents helps support identify the correct issue.

After delivery, do not let a long tracking discussion delay an eligible after-sales request. Public Hacoo guidance commonly refers to a 15-day window after receipt, but eligibility and instructions depend on the current order terms and product category. Check the deadline shown for your order promptly. Follow the instructions given for that case before shipping anything back; different official pages describe different return scenarios.
## Send one concise evidence package
A useful support message contains the order reference, the affected parcel, last tracking event with its date, the item involved and the action requested. For example: ask support to confirm the carrier handover or investigate a delivered scan, rather than demand that a generic status be changed. Add only the screenshots needed to show the problem. Redact unrelated personal information and keep unredacted documents within the authenticated support process when required.

Save the response and the next review date. If new evidence appears, update the existing case with the changed fact. Do not place another order merely because a tracking page is quiet, and do not cancel a case just because an unrelated parcel arrived. The useful outcome is a clear record of which shipment needs action, who is handling it and what remains unresolved. Hacoo VIP cannot access orders or trace parcels; this guide helps you prepare a more effective request to the service responsible for yours.

## A worked parcel record
Consider a hypothetical order containing a hoodie and a bag. The hoodie has a delivered event, while the bag has a different tracking number whose last event describes a carrier handover. Write two separate rows: item, parcel number, last event, event date, expected interval and next action. The hoodie row can move to an item inspection; the bag row still needs a shipment update. Do not copy the delivered status into both rows merely because they share an order number. If support later confirms a new carrier number for the bag, add it to that row and preserve the earlier reference. This example does not describe a real customer order. Its purpose is to keep evidence and actions attached to the right shipment, particularly when one order contains products with different dispatch schedules.
