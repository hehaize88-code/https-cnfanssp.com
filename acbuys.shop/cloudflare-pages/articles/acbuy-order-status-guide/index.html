ACBuy Order Status: From Purchased to Stored, Step by Step

An order status describes one stage of the purchasing and warehouse process. Read it alongside the order details before deciding whether to wait, check an action or contact support.

Research note

The status sequence comes from ACBuy’s official Shopping Guide: Under review, Purchased, Seller Sent, Delivered, Being Stored and Stored. The explanations below distinguish that published sequence from practical troubleshooting advice; no fixed processing time is promised.

01

Begin with the correct order record

Open the exact order rather than relying on a general account notification. Match its source product, selected size or colour, quantity and purchase date with your saved choice. If several similar products were ordered, use the identifier to keep them separate. One order can progress while another remains at an earlier stage. An account-level impression that everything is delayed may simply combine different sellers or products with different histories.

Record the current status, the time of the last visible update and any action requested in the order. Check whether the account shows an unread message about stock, specifications or a price difference. These details are more useful than repeatedly refreshing the list. A status is a summary label; the associated message may explain what needs to happen next. Do not supply additional payment or change an option until you understand which order and charge the request concerns.

02

Read Under review as a purchasing checkpoint

Under review appears at the beginning of the official order sequence. Treat it as a reason to inspect the submitted information, not as evidence that the seller has dispatched the product. Ensure that the source link still identifies the intended listing and that size, colour, quantity and remarks are unambiguous. A manually entered order deserves particular attention because the purchasing instructions depend on the information supplied by the buyer.

If clarification is requested, answer the actual question with a measurable choice. A size code or listed option is clearer than a request for the best version. Keep a record of any agreed change so it can be compared with the warehouse item later. The public status sequence does not establish a universal review deadline. Use the current order information and any response from the assigned agent to assess the particular case instead of borrowing another buyer’s timeline.

03

Distinguish Purchased from Seller Sent

Purchased and Seller Sent are separate labels in the official flow. Purchased should not be treated as proof of international dispatch, or even as a substitute for the later seller-shipment information. The important next check is whether the order details show progress towards domestic delivery. Review messages about availability or changed specifications before assuming that the original listing remains unchanged. Keep all decisions tied to that order rather than to a general product recommendation.

When the status reaches Seller Sent, look for the domestic delivery information available in the account. That movement concerns the seller-to-warehouse stage. It is not the same shipment as the eventual parcel to your home. If domestic tracking exists, compare its latest event with the order status; the two systems may update at different moments. A missing update alone does not identify who caused a delay. Ask about the specific stage and available evidence.

04

Separate Delivered from Being Stored

A domestic delivery event and a completed warehouse record are not interchangeable. A package may have reached its domestic destination while receiving, matching, inspection or storage information is still being processed. The official sequence includes Delivered and Being Stored before Stored. Therefore, do not conclude that an item is ready for an international parcel merely because a domestic carrier records delivery. Check whether the warehouse entry itself is available.

If the domestic record says delivered but the account does not show the expected warehouse item, gather the order identifier, domestic tracking reference, visible delivery event and last account status. Check for multiple packages or quantities before reporting a missing item. Send a concise inquiry asking the agent to reconcile those records. Avoid guessing about warehouse handling from a status label alone. The useful question is which recorded item or package still needs to be matched or processed.

05

Use Stored as the start of your QC decision

Stored is a warehouse milestone, not automatic approval of the product. Open the item’s photographs and any recorded measurements. Compare the received colour, variant, quantity and included accessories with the order choice. Look at the whole item before zooming into details. If a crucial measurement or angle is absent, check the available options for more information. A clean-looking thumbnail cannot establish fit, construction or every aspect of product condition.

Note the storage deadline displayed for the individual item and review any after-sales options promptly. A warehouse storage allowance does not establish the seller’s return window. Waiting for several other products before opening QC photos can leave less time to deal with a mismatch. Keep the decision separate from the status: an item may be stored while you are still evaluating it. Include it in a parcel only after the available evidence supports your decision to keep it.

06

Diagnose a stalled status with a short timeline

Build a timeline from observable events: order submitted, last message, current status, seller tracking event and warehouse evidence where available. Include dates and the time zone if timestamps come from different systems. Compare this information with the particular estimate or instruction currently shown in the account. The official status names alone do not provide a fixed number of hours for every transition, so a universal waiting chart would give false confidence.

Then identify the missing event. If no seller shipment is recorded, an international carrier cannot explain that gap. If domestic delivery is recorded but storage is incomplete, focus the inquiry on receiving and order matching. If the item is already stored, look for QC or parcel actions rather than waiting for the purchase label to change again. This stage-based approach narrows the question and avoids sending the same vague complaint to several unrelated parties.

07

Send an inquiry with a clear requested action

ACBuy’s guide points buyers to Order Inquiry during the purchasing process. Use the relevant account channel and include the exact order number, current status, last visible change and any conflicting delivery evidence. State what you need confirmed: seller dispatch, a changed option, warehouse receipt or an explanation of a requested adjustment. A short description with the right identifiers is easier to investigate than a large collection of unrelated screenshots.

For example, describe that the domestic carrier shows delivery on a stated date while the order still shows an earlier stage, and ask for a check of warehouse receipt. This is an illustrative message structure, not a report about an actual customer order. Remove unnecessary payment details and personal information from images shared outside the account. Save the response and any agreed next action, then follow that instruction instead of opening duplicate requests with inconsistent information.

08

Move from an order to an international parcel

After inspection and storage, selecting goods for international shipment creates a separate parcel decision. Review the warehouse items, quantities, packaging, destination and eligible shipping options. Keep the parcel identifier alongside the included order identifiers. This mapping helps when a parcel contains products from several purchases or when some stored items remain behind. A purchase order and a consolidated parcel can represent different groups of goods.

Once an international parcel is submitted, use its own status and tracking information for the next stage. Do not expect the original seller’s tracking number to explain overseas movement. Continue with the parcel-tracking guide for carrier handoffs and missing updates, and use the return guide if warehouse inspection reveals a problem. Understanding which record controls each stage makes the account easier to use and keeps support questions specific from purchase through delivery.
