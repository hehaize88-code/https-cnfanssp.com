How to Check ACBuy Shoe QC Photos Before Shipping

Review the pair in a fixed order: identity and size, matching shape, construction details, soles, included pieces and visible damage. Ask for evidence when an angle is missing.

Updated September 9, 20269 min read

Research note

This checklist explains what warehouse photographs can visibly support. It does not claim authenticity, material composition, fit, comfort or durability from images, and it does not replace the seller’s current after-sales terms.

01

Match the shoes to the exact order first

Open the saved product record and confirm the selected model, color, size system, quantity and included accessories. Then compare those facts with the warehouse order and every photograph. Size numbers can refer to different regional systems, so do not approve from the number alone when a seller chart or requested insole measurement is available. Check that both shoes belong to the same pair and that the color blocking, materials visible in the listing and major design panels match the chosen option.

This identity check is more important than a tiny stitch variation. A visually clean pair in the wrong size or color is still the wrong order. Save the source identifier and screenshot of the selected option beside the warehouse images. If the listing changed after purchase, rely on the order record created at submission. Do not use a spreadsheet thumbnail as the final specification because it may represent another variant or an older seller image.

02

Compare the pair as a pair

Begin with a straight overhead or front view showing both shoes together. Compare overall length, toe shape, panel height, lace placement and left-to-right symmetry. Then use rear and side views to compare heel height, collar shape and alignment of large panels. Some apparent differences come from camera angle, stuffing or how the shoes are placed, so look across several images before deciding that the pair is mismatched.

Ask whether the difference would remain if both shoes were positioned equally. A tilted shoe can make the toe appear wider, while uneven lacing can change the upper shape. Genuine visible concerns include clearly different cuts, asymmetric attachment, a twisted upper or a major panel placed differently on one side. Mark the exact area in a support request and ask for a level comparison image if the standard photographs cannot settle it.

  • Check both size labels.
  • Compare toes, heels and collar height.
  • Account for camera angle and stuffing.
  • Request a matched view when symmetry is unclear.
03

Inspect stitching, joins and adhesive

Zoom into the perimeter where the upper meets the midsole, around panel edges and near high-stress bends. Look for open seams, skipped stitches, loose long threads, detached edges or a gap that appears structural. Small thread ends or limited visible adhesive can be cosmetic, while widespread separation deserves closer evidence. Compare the same area on left and right shoes and distinguish a light reflection from a physical gap.

Warehouse photography can reveal surface workmanship but not internal bonding strength. Do not declare long-term durability from one close-up. If a suspected defect is visible only in a compressed image, request a focused photo under neutral lighting and state the location. Decide based on the current seller return or exchange option, product value and your tolerance for the observed issue. Avoid vague requests for the agent to confirm premium quality because quality is not a measurable instruction.

04

Check labels and measurements without overclaiming

Review the visible size tag, box label when retained and any model or color notation that can be matched to the order. Labels can help identify a wrong variant, but they do not establish authenticity. If fit is important, a measured insole or outsole can be more useful than a printed size. Follow the platform’s current photo or measurement options and specify the exact endpoints needed so the result can be compared with a shoe that fits.

Measurements taken through a photograph can contain small error from tape angle, shoe curvature or padding. Use them as evidence within a tolerance rather than claiming millimetre certainty. Also check that the pair is measured consistently. If the seller chart and physical measure differ materially, document both before the after-sales deadline. Shipping the pair first removes the easiest opportunity to resolve an obvious size mismatch while it remains in the warehouse.

Best fit evidence

Compare a clearly requested physical measurement with a shoe you already wear successfully. The printed size alone cannot guarantee fit.

05

Review outsole, midsole and contact surfaces

Request or use a clear sole view to check that both outsole patterns match, the shoes appear unused and no obvious section is detached or deeply damaged. Inspect midsole edges for large dents, deformation and inconsistent joins. Dust or a warehouse-floor mark may be superficial, while abrasion across contact points can suggest wear. Use several images before labeling a shoe used, because lighting and protective film can alter surface appearance.

For structured footwear, examine whether the sole sits flat and the upper appears centred. A photograph cannot test traction, cushioning or internal support. It can show visible warping or a strongly uneven attachment. If the only sole image is angled or partly covered, ask for a direct view. Keep the question specific: identify the area and request confirmation through a photograph, not an unsupported verbal guarantee about construction or performance.

06

Confirm accessories and shipping choices

Check the order for spare laces, dust bags, tags, inserts or other pieces that were explicitly included. Warehouse images may not show small accessories unless arranged separately, so compare the item count and ask before approval. Decide whether the retail shoe box is useful. Keeping it can protect structure and preserve labels, but it adds volume and may raise shipping cost. Removing it can reduce dimensions but increases the need for suitable alternative protection.

Choose packaging after the QC decision, not before. If the pair has delicate shape, hardware or surface finish, describe the protection needed. If compact shipping is the priority, compare the route’s volumetric rule and the measured parcel with and without the box when possible. Do not assume box removal always saves a fixed amount. The effect depends on the rest of the parcel, final dimensions, selected line and its current billing method.

07

Make a documented accept-or-resolve decision

Summarise the review in five lines: correct identity and size, pair symmetry, construction observations, sole and label check, accessories and damage. Classify each concern as clearly acceptable, needing evidence or requiring an eligible after-sales request. This prevents a minor cosmetic mark from hiding a larger mismatch and helps support understand the requested outcome. Keep the product out of parcel submission while any material question remains open.

Remember the boundary of shoe QC photos. They can support visible condition and order accuracy; they cannot prove authenticity, exact material composition, comfort, fit on your foot or long-term wear. If the evidence supports acceptance, save the key images until delivery. If it does not, act within the current seller and platform timeline. A consistent checklist produces a better decision than chasing an online verdict based on one cropped frame.
